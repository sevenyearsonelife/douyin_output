Want to be a better software architect?Here is a hot take: start by learning English. Hear me out. Hey friends, I'm No Land. Recently I made a video on Spring Boot architecture. To keep it short, I titled the first chapter "Are you really familiar with SB?

" Well, my comment section went crazy. People thought I was swearing at them, because in Chinese, SB means, well, idiot. But it's literally just short for Spring Boot. This funny mistake actually shows a huge blind spot for us. We are trying to brute force an English-based coding with a purely Chinese mindset. Think about it. Java, Go, they are all built on English. To master them, it all comes down to one magic word: familiarity. Take variable naming for example. If you have a little familiarity with English, you will stop writing those weird Pinyin and English mixes. Like, ah, look at this database from a friend. When you see these table names, do you really want to read his code?

It's a massive red flag. And what about error logs?

I've seen devs stare at a giant null pointer exception, and instead of fixing it, they throw it into translate APIs. The API turns a simple null pointer into some deep, confusing Chinese words. Then they just throw their hands and say, man, this error makes zero sense. Look, we've spent years studying English grammar for exams, and it was truly painful. But what if you just try to speak it?

English isn't a boss fight you need to beat. Listen to some English songs, watch movies, and when you hit a weird variable name, just look up how to say it and repeat it out loud. That's how you build familiarity. When you have that familiarity, reading docs or writing code becomes second nature. Honestly, reading official English docs beats watching secondhand tutorials any day of the week. But have you ever actually tried it?

And you know what?

Architecture is exactly the same. It's all about familiarity. When people hear architecture, they instantly think of huge systems or microservices. They make it sound like rocket science, but it's really not. Architecture is just like the English you're ever learning. It's right in front of you. Every framework you use, every open source project you check, even a simple helper class you write, they all have architecture baked in. You don't need to read heavy architecture books. You just need to build the same familiarity with your code. Once you have that familiarity, you will naturally start asking questions while coding. Why did they lay it like this?

Or if I get a new feature tomorrow, is this API easy to change?

It's that simple. You don't have to force it. Just bring this mindset into your daily coding. Use a few small tricks and your code quality will quietly skyrocket. And listen, this is not just a Java thing. This familiarity is your secret weapon for any tech out there. Especially right now with AI blowing up. If you build the familiarity with how AI models think and work, you will never fall behind. Now, do you get that familiarity?

I'm No Land, let's level up together.
